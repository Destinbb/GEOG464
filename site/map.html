Montreal: Bicycle Network

← Back to home

Bicycle commuting offers a relatively low-cost, healthy and environmentally friendly alternative to motorized transportation. However, for people who are new to cycling or unfamiliar with Montreal, navigating the city's cycling infrastructure can be intimidating. Furthermore, protected bike routes that offer a physical barrier between the bike lane and vehicle traffic can provide a safer commuting experience. Understanding where bike routes are located and which types of infrastructure they offer can help people make more informed decisions about their commute.

This map is for: Cyclists in Montreal who may not be familiar with the city's bike-lane infrastructure.

What you can do here: Press Locate me to see where you are on the map, or type a start and end address to plan a bike route from A to B. Click a road segment to see its details. Compare how separated from cars each route is by turning the lane layers on and off, and tick Year-round routes only to see what you can ride in winter. Popup information for each segment explains its type, area, comfort level, seasonal status, and recorded length. Users can also click a pavement segment for its pavement score out of 100 (the city's Pavement Condition Index) and survey date. The pavement rating describes the surveyed street segment matched to this route by the city's street segment number; it may not represent the bike lane’s surface specifically as the data is from 2024.

Lane types:

  • Separated from cars (thick solid dark blue): a curb, barrier or separate path between you and traffic. Multi-use trails are shared with people walking.
  • Painted lane (medium solid light blue): your own lane, but only paint between you and cars.
  • Shared with traffic (thin dashed pink): no lane of your own; you ride in the street with cars or buses. Off at the start.

I used GitHub Copilot with gpt-6-luna to help load and clean GeoJSON data from Données Québec's open-data portal. I used the agent to filter the scope of the data by only keeping features where VILLE_MTL = Oui. I later used Claude Code to widen this to every route on the island of Montreal, including the linked municipalities such as Westmount and Dorval. I also used the agent to remove features with missing geometry or required fields, count the exclusions and translate the selected property keys from French to English for the map. Finally I used the agent to split the data into protected and unprotected layers. I reviewed the filtered GeoJSON and made sure that only routes on the island of Montreal and with required fields were displayed on the map. I also checked that the English translations preserved the meaning of the original values. Finally, I confirmed that all segments were correctly classified into the protected and unprotected layers, and were displayed correctly on the map. I also tested the page by toggling the visibility of each layer and opening the popups to ensure they displayed the feature information.
